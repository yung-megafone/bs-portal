Policy

Privacy first.

B.S. Portal is designed to collect what operations require, not what is merely available.

B.S. Portal Alpha · Last updated September 5, 2026

Reference deployment: This policy describes the B.S. Portal software and the intended B.S. Supply Co. deployment. Because BSP is open source and self-hostable, another operator can configure hosting, logging, integrations, access, and retention differently and is responsible for documenting those choices.

Core privacy principles

  • No advertising or ad-tech tracking.
  • No sale of portal data.
  • No third-party behavioral analytics by default.
  • No covert employee/location surveillance features.
  • Keep operational data first-party and self-hostable.
  • Prefer explicit records and accountable history over invisible profiling.

What BSP processes

Identity: account identifiers, authentication state, staff roles, and department memberships required to operate authenticated workflows.

BAM: asset identity, type, manufacturer/model/serial, department ownership, custodian, evidence, custody/lifecycle history, and asset-use requests. Asset requests can include requester identity, project/purpose, requested date windows, preferred resources, waitlist/reservation state, justification, and optional related SHIT work.

SHIT: requester and assignment information, ticket text, severity/status, comments, internal notes, attachments, related assets/documents, event history, and queue position.

Timeclock: employee identity, clock-in/out timestamps, punch source, corrections, correction reasons, and related audit events.

The current Timeclock module is intentionally not designed to collect GPS location, biometrics, device fingerprints, advertising identifiers, or continuous-presence telemetry.

Cookies, logs, and telemetry

BSP uses the session and CSRF state required by Django for authenticated operation and request protection. These are functional/security mechanisms, not advertising cookies. The application does not require a third-party analytics or advertising SDK.

A deployment's web server, reverse proxy, host, operating system, database, or security tooling may still create technical logs such as IP addresses, timestamps, paths, browser user agents, errors, or authentication events. Those logs are controlled by the deployment operator.

How information is used

Operational records are used to authenticate users, enforce access, run department workflows, maintain assets, manage service tickets and resource reservations, record work-time punches, preserve accountable history, diagnose failures, protect the service, and perform authorized backup/recovery. BSP is not designed for advertising, cross-service profiling, or sale to data brokers.

Sharing and storage

BSP does not require sharing records with advertisers. Data may be accessible to authorized portal users, deployment administrators, configured infrastructure/storage providers, or parties entitled to information through applicable legal process. Third-party integrations that receive BSP data should be documented explicitly.

The reference architecture uses MySQL/InnoDB and application-controlled media storage. Self-hosted operators determine where databases, uploads, logs, and backups physically reside and should protect backups as copies of the same operational data.

Retention, correction, and deletion

BSP contains history-oriented records such as custody events, ticket events, time punches, and corrections. The alpha software does not currently impose one universal retention schedule. Operators should define retention according to operational, contractual, legal, and security requirements.

Where preserving history is important, BSP may retain the original record and append a correction instead of silently rewriting history. Requests that cannot be handled in the portal should be directed to the operator of the specific BSP instance.

Open-source privacy

Downloading, cloning, building, or running the B.S. Portal source code does not intentionally contact B.S. Supply Co. for telemetry or usage analytics. A fork can change that behavior, so users of third-party deployments should review the operator's own policy and configuration.

Security and changes

Privacy depends on security. See the BSP Security Policy for current alpha limitations and deployment expectations.

Material privacy-impacting changes should be documented alongside the corresponding code or deployment change.
